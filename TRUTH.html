    fish (2026)

    fish (2026) is a piece about the past, the present, and the future. The artist represents this passage of time by the degradation of the tape lessening as the piece advances. As the tape becomes less degraded, the image becomes clearer, making it both more normal and less visually interesting. This is a commentary on how historical events are often mythologized and distorted as time passes, while current future historical events are normalized. This theme is heightened by the artist’s use of old VHS technology, technology that was normal twenty to thirty years ago but has since been similarly mythologized by internet trends such as the analog horror boom of the early 2020s.

    Also of significance to the artist is the method of degradation. Though modern technology can easily reproduce the effect digitally, the artist opts to record from one VHS tape to another to achieve the effect. This adds an additional layer to the work; while the resurrection of the fish represents time, the glitches and imperfections are visual artifacts from the process of creation.

    There is also an element of Duchampian authorship present in the work. The fish is a found image, The visual artifacts were not placed there by the artist with intentionality, nor did the artist make the television which the piece was displayed on during documentation. The only thing that the artist did was record things that were already there. Does he deserve authorship any more than the photographer, or the television manufacturer, or, for that matter, the fish? Or should he be placed before a firing squad?


     ------------------------------------------ Mom Found the Punch Drawer (2026)

    In 1818, Mary Shelly published “Frankenstein; or, The Modern Prometheus,” in which scientist Victor Frankenstein manages to creates life, and upon doing so, is suddenly reviled at the object of his obsession being realized. The artist describes a similar thought process throughout the creation of this piece. This work is an examination of hubris. During the process, the artist deployed many different tactics in his desperation to prevent the filing cabinet from leaking all over the place. None of these efforts were ultimately successful. Although the original piece and all of the liquid contained were supposedly destroyed shortly after creation, the artist claims that some of the contents escaped destruction and are plotting revenge. Regardless of the artist’s delusions, there is no credible evidence of this.

    The piece is part of a series of works by the artist satirizing corporate culture. Lumpy red liquid fills the top of a filing cabinet. The title implies that this is punch. From Merriam Webster: “punch(noun): a hot or cold drink that is usually a combination of hard liquor, wine, or beer and nonalcoholic beverages; also: a drink that is a mixture of nonalcoholic beverages.” That definition encompasses every combination of drinkable liquids in existence, so there’s really no way of telling what is in this. One might imagine it is a secret kept close to the chest of a haggard employee, their only solace through a dull and dismal workday. One might imagine, at night, it takes on a life of its own, sitting at a desk, typing, shredding paper, gathering at the water cooler. One must imagine the punch drawer happy.
